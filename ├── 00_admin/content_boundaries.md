# Content Boundaries

> **Campaign:** Candy Mountain Massacre  
> **Status:** Active table agreement  
> **Last updated:** YYYY-MM-DD

---

## Default Intensity

Candy Mountain Massacre is a serious modern-fantasy horror campaign.

The default tone allows:

- Graphic candy-body horror.
- Gore, rot, spoilage, mold, melting, cracking, exposed filling, and industrial processing imagery.
- Corpses, dead workers, missing climbers, and disturbing aftermath scenes.
- Body transformation, magical contamination, and failed Jawbreaker return consequences.
- Cannibalism as a serious horror subject.
- Corporate exploitation, worker abuse, dehumanizing “Ingredients” language, and industrial cruelty.
- Monsters, traps, violence, severe injuries, death, and permanent consequences.
- Dark comedy, corporate absurdity, strange broadcasts, and unsettling candy-world details.
- Fear, grief, paranoia, desperation, betrayal, and moral pressure.

Candy materials change the imagery. They do not make violence emotionally harmless.

---

## Core Limits

The campaign does not include:

- Sexual violence or sexual coercion.
- Sexual content involving minors.
- Explicit sexual content as a focus of play.
- Real-world hate speech or prejudice used for entertainment.
- Harm to player characters that removes their agency solely for shock value.

If a subject becomes more intense than the group wants, reduce detail, fade the scene, change direction, or skip it.

---

## Adjustment Tools

Anyone may use any of these at any time, without explanation:

- **Pause:** Stop and check the scene.
- **Fade:** Skip graphic description and move past the moment.
- **Veil:** Keep the subject in the fiction, but do not describe it directly.
- **Reframe:** Change the scene’s focus, tone, or method of presentation.
- **Skip:** Remove the subject or scene entirely.
- **Rewind:** Briefly revise a detail that crossed a boundary.

No player must justify using an adjustment tool.

---

## GM Guidance

The GM should:

- Use gore when it creates atmosphere, clues, consequence, or real emotional impact.
- Avoid turning every room into a gore display.
- Use quiet, clean, strange, comic, social, and beautiful scenes for contrast.
- Never use graphic horror to force a player-character decision.
- Preserve player agency during violence, transformation, captivity, fear, and death.
- Ask a quick check-in if a scene becomes unusually intense, personal, or prolonged.
- Treat character death, Jawbreaker return, and permanent loss as meaningful—not cheap punishment.
